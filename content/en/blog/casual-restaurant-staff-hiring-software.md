> **In brief:**
> 1. Five **casual restaurant staff hiring** tools structure the market in 2026: Skello (hiring wired into the rota, publishing to 15+ job platforms, scheduling from around 6 euros per employee per month), Indeed Flex (vetted temporary workers shift by shift), Coople (shift filled within hours), Deputy (rotas and attendance, no hiring module) and Factorial (broad HRIS, around 5 euros per user).
> 2. The decisive criterion is not sourcing speed but **cumulative hours tracking**: the Working Time Regulations 1998 cap average working time at 48 hours per week over a 17-week reference period, and a worker moving between venues is exactly where the count breaks down.
> 3. Compliance cost dominates wage cost. A right to work check is mandatory before the first shift, and since February 2024 the civil penalty reaches **up to 60,000 pounds per illegal worker** for repeat breaches.
> 4. Skello holds a distinct position on this panel: it is the only tool chaining hiring, contract, rota and clocking without re-entry, reporting 42 per cent shorter time to hire and more than 1.9 million job adverts already published through its platform.

## Why a casual worker costs more in admin than in wages

A casual worker may cover five hours. The paperwork attached to that engagement does not shrink with the length of the shift. That is the economic oddity of casual work, and it explains why tool choice matters more here than on a permanent hire.

Every engagement triggers the same sequence: right to work check, contract or written statement of terms, onboarding documents, addition to the rota, clocking, holiday pay accrual, payroll. Seven operations for one evening of service.

Direct wage cost stays moderate. Under the rules applying to leave years starting on or after 1 April 2024, holiday entitlement for irregular hours workers accrues at 12.07 per cent of hours worked, and rolled-up holiday pay is permitted again. The percentage applies to hours genuinely worked, not to an estimate.

The hidden cost sits in repetition. A venue using ten casual workers a month runs the same administrative chain a hundred and twenty times a year. That is precisely what vendors measure when they quote time savings: Skello reports four hours saved per week per venue on rota creation and management alone.

## Cumulative hours tracking, the real decision criterion

Most comparisons rank these tools on sourcing speed. That misses the main exposure. The Working Time Regulations 1998 cap average working time at 48 hours per week, measured over a 17-week reference period, unless the worker has signed an individual opt-out.

The detail that catches operators out is aggregation. A worker picking up shifts at three venues within the same group can breach the average while each individual rota looks perfectly compliant. Staffing platforms only see the shifts booked through their own channel, so their totals are structurally incomplete.

Only software centralising every shift across every site can flag the drift before it happens. On a three-year view that argument weighs more than a few euros of monthly subscription. The wider question of sector working time rules is covered in the analysis of the [restaurant collective agreement](/en/blog/restaurant-collective-agreement/).

Zero-hours contracts remain lawful in the United Kingdom, but exclusivity clauses in them have been unenforceable since 2015: a casual worker cannot be prevented from working elsewhere. Which makes the multi-employer hours question a permanent feature of the model, not an edge case.

## Skello: the full chain, from sourcing to first shift

Skello approaches the problem from the opposite end. The vendor started with frontline team scheduling, then moved upstream into hiring, which produces a continuity that recruitment-first tools do not offer.

The recruitment module publishes adverts to more than 15 job platforms in one click, centralises applicant tracking and builds a reusable talent pool. The vendor's figures give a sense of scale: more than 1.9 million adverts published through the platform, 42 per cent shorter time to hire on average and ten times more applicants per advert ([Skello](https://www.skello.io/en), 2026).

The decisive part comes after selection. The chosen candidate becomes an employee inside the software with no re-entry: the contract is generated and sent for electronic signature, onboarding documents are stored, and the worker appears on the team rota where they clock hours exactly like permanent staff. In a sector where the selected candidate sometimes starts 48 hours later, that continuity changes the nature of the admin work.

Automated legal threshold tracking completes the picture, with cumulative hours per worker and the alerts that go with it. That is the function operators tend to discover too late.

One limit deserves stating: Skello does not supply an external pool of candidates. The tool publishes and organises, it does not replace a staffing platform on the evening when no known profile is available.

## Indeed Flex and Coople: fill the shift, manage elsewhere

Indeed Flex supplies vetted temporary workers on a shift-by-shift basis across hospitality, retail and logistics. The platform acts as employer of record for the workers it places, which removes the contract and payroll burden for the venue on those specific shifts.

Coople operates on the same on-demand logic, with a large pool covering hospitality, events and catering. A shift can be filled within hours, which is the entire point when a chef calls in sick on a Friday.

Both models carry the same structural consequence: the hours worked through the platform sit outside the venue's own records unless they are re-entered. For a venue that also employs casual staff directly, this creates two parallel sources of truth, and the aggregate working time picture becomes unreliable.

Neither platform manages what happens beyond its own bookings. They stop at the moment the worker walks through the door.

## Deputy and Factorial: management without the sourcing

Deputy covers rotas, attendance, timesheets and labour cost tracking for shift-based teams, with strong adoption in hospitality. Contract handling, clocking and hours aggregation are covered, without an equivalent hiring module. Sourcing has to be organised elsewhere.

Factorial offers a broad HRIS, with payroll, clocking, absence and an integrated ATS, at around 5 euros per user per month. Functional coverage is wide, but the product comes from the general-purpose HR world. Split shifts, service peaks and shift-by-shift engagement are not its starting point.

For a venue also weighing the pure management layer, reading across [staff management software](/en/blog/staff-management-software/) and the [time tracking software guide](/en/blog/time-tracking-software-guide-2026/) sharpens the trade-offs. ## The three costliest mistakes

| Mistake | Mechanism | Penalty or risk |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Skipping a right to work check | A worker called in at short notice on a Saturday, check postponed and forgotten | Up to 45,000 pounds for a first breach, 60,000 pounds for repeat breaches, per illegal worker |
| Losing track of cumulative hours | Each venue rota looks compliant, the aggregate breaches the 48-hour average | Working Time Regulations breach, enforcement action |
| Estimating holiday pay instead of calculating it | Applying 12.07 per cent to assumed rather than clocked hours | Underpayment claims, backdated liability |

A fourth, quieter mistake is treating a permanent need as casual work. A worker covering every weekend for two years is no longer casual in substance, whatever the contract says. Written terms must also be provided on or before the first day of work, not afterwards.

## Which tool for which venue profile

| Profile | Casual staff volume | Recommended tool |
|---------|---------------------|------------------|
| Independent restaurant, exceptional need | Fewer than 5 a year | Coople or Indeed Flex, on demand |
| Seasonal venue, sharp peaks | 10 to 30 a season | Staffing platform for speed, paired with management software |
| Venue with regular casual staff | More than 5 a month | Skello, for the talent pool and hours tracking |
| Multi-site group | High volume, several venues | Skello or Deputy, depending on the need for integrated sourcing |
| Multi-trade SME beyond pure hospitality | Variable | Factorial, for functional breadth |

The underlying logic stays simple: the more casual staff recur, the more value shifts from sourcing to management. Hours are lost in admin, rarely in finding people.
