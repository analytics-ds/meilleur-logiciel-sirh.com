**Absence management software** centralizes the declaration, approval and tracking of every employee absence, whether planned or unplanned. It covers a broader scope than a simple leave tool: sick leave, workplace accidents, unexcused absences and administrative supporting documents all live in the same place.

## What is absence management software

Absence management software logs every interruption in an employee's activity, distinguishing absences planned in advance from unplanned events. It differs from [leave management software](/en/blog/leave-management-software/) in scope: the latter mainly handles paid leave and RTT days, while an absence management tool also covers sick leave, workplace accidents and unexcused lateness.

The main goal stays the same in both cases: replacing shared spreadsheets or email exchanges with a structured flow, fully traceable for HR and payroll. The real difference lies in the type of supporting documents to collect and the legal declaration deadlines, which are stricter for sick leave than for a standard leave request.

## The types of absences to cover

A complete tool distinguishes several absence categories, each with its own declaration and justification rules. **Sick leave** requires submitting a work stoppage notice within a regulatory deadline, with a direct impact on the payroll subrogation calculation. A **workplace or commuting accident** follows a specific declarative procedure with the health insurance fund, which the tool must be able to track with its own deadlines.

**Unexcused absences** are a special case: the software must alert the manager in real time so they can react quickly, before the situation repeats itself. Finally, family events (marriage, bereavement, birth) and standard leave remain managed in parallel, often through the same module or through a connected [leave management tool](/en/blog/leave-management-software/).

## Key features of absence management software

Four features consistently appear across market solutions. **Online declaration** lets the employee or manager report an absence in a few clicks, with direct document upload from a mobile phone. **Automatic payroll impact calculation** applies the absence to the payslip (subrogation, waiting period, salary continuation) without any manual re-entry by the payroll team.

**Absenteeism tracking** aggregates data by department, absence type and period, with configurable alert thresholds to spot an abnormal rate before it becomes structural. **Scheduling integration** automatically reflects an approved absence on already published shifts, a critical point for field teams that rely daily on [scheduling software](/en/blog/employee-scheduling-software/).

Some tools add a module for mandatory medical visits or a direct connection with [time and attendance software](/en/blog/best-time-attendance-software-2026/) to cross-check clock-in data against declared absences, a useful check to keep actual working time calculations reliable.

## Free absence management software: what actually changes

Several vendors offer a free plan limited in user count or configurable absence types, suitable for a very small structure with simple rules. These versions cover basic declarations but quickly hit limits once several collective agreements need to be managed or missing-document reminders need to be automated.

The most frequent limitation concerns the payroll impact calculation: free versions often apply a generic subrogation rule rather than the exact provisions of the applicable agreement. HR then has to manually check each case, which cancels out much of the time saved at the start.

A spreadsheet remains a common free alternative for very small teams, but it triggers no automatic alert for unexcused absences and does not centrally archive supporting documents. Switching to a dedicated tool generally becomes worthwhile once a company passes about ten employees or manages several absence types in parallel.

## Comparing absence management software

The table below compares the main solutions available on the French market, without any ranking: the right choice depends on the range of absences to cover and the tools already in place.

| Software | Positioning | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Lucca (Figgo) | Modular HRIS | Fine-grained multi-agreement subrogation rules |
| Eurécia | General HRIS | Absence module built into a full HRIS |
| Skello | Field scheduling | Absences reflected directly on the schedule |
| Factorial | General HRIS | Simple interface, good feature-to-price ratio |
| Jibble | Time tracking | Free plan with unlimited users |
| Combo | Scheduling and payroll | Automated absence export to payroll |

This comparison remains indicative: pricing and feature scope change regularly, testing against the company's actual use cases remains necessary before any commitment.

Two criteria help narrow this choice quickly. The first is the diversity of absence reasons to handle: a company mostly dealing with sick leave and recurring absenteeism should prioritize a tool strong on subrogation calculations and threshold alerts. The second is whether a direct link with scheduling is needed: field teams with variable hours benefit from a solution that automatically reflects every absence on already published shifts.

## How to choose absence management software by company size

A small business with stable rules can be satisfied with an absence module built into a general HRIS, without needing a dedicated tool. The deciding factor then becomes ease of declaration rather than depth of configuration for absence reasons.

A mid-sized or larger multi-site company, on the other hand, benefits from prioritizing a tool able to manage several collective agreements and a high volume of absences in parallel, with automated payroll export. This applies to organizations already using [time tracking software](/en/blog/time-tracking-software-guide-2026/) to track actual working time: the absence module then needs to integrate natively to avoid discrepancies between clock-in data and declarations.

A company looking to replace a tool that has become too rigid, for example after comparing [Kelio alternatives](/en/blog/kelio-alternatives/), should first check how absenteeism history is carried over during migration: losing this history makes it harder to track HR indicators over time.

The level of integration with payroll and scheduling matters as much as the absence module's own feature depth. A standalone tool that exchanges no data with these two systems eventually recreates the double entry the software was meant to eliminate in the first place.
