## The two handoffs that decide the real time saving

Most comparisons stop at checking whether all three functions exist. That answer is incomplete, because a platform can display three modules and move no data between them automatically.

Administrative time is not lost inside a function. It is lost precisely where a piece of data has to cross a boundary: when a candidate becomes an employee, and when a worked hour becomes a payslip line. Those are the two handoffs, and they are the only criterion that genuinely separates these platforms.

**Recruiting, scheduling and time tracking software** is therefore worth exactly what its two relay points are worth. Everything else, the number of reports, the breadth of the module list, the presence of a conversational assistant, is measured afterwards.

## Handoff 1: from accepted application to first scheduled shift

Five pieces of information have to cross this boundary. Contract type, hourly rate, assigned site, start date and the availability the person has stated.

When the handoff is native, accepting an application creates the employee record, generates the contract, triggers the statutory hiring declaration where one applies, and makes the person appear in the schedule with their constraints attached. When it is not native, those five fields are retyped by hand, often by someone other than the person who did the hiring, with the error risk that implies on an hourly rate or a start date.

The gap scales with hiring volume. A site that hires four times a year will never notice. A retail store that renews twenty people per season runs the same copy sequence forty times a year, which is what vendors quantify when they claim hours saved: Skello reports around four hours per week per site on schedule creation and management alone.

Planday illustrates a handoff that is absent by design. Its product covers AI-powered rotas, time tracking, payroll accuracy, a communication hub and compliance, and it does that well, but there is no applicant tracking upstream. Sourcing happens elsewhere and the data comes across manually.

BambooHR shows the mirror image. Its applicant tracking system and onboarding are among the most established on the market, and its payroll and reporting are solid, but it has no shift scheduling at all. The first handoff works and there is simply nothing on the other side of it for a shift-based team.

## Handoff 2: from clocked hours to payroll variables

The second handoff is the expensive one when it is missing, because it repeats every month rather than at every hire.

Four data types have to pass through: actual hours worked, overtime premiums, absences with their reason codes, and variable pay such as bonuses or allowances. An export in the format the payroll provider or accountant expects removes an entire monthly re-entry. A generic spreadsheet export simply moves that re-entry from one desk to another.

This handoff is also where the difference between scheduled and actual hours becomes concrete. A platform that pushes the theoretical roster into payroll detects neither end-of-shift overruns nor breaks that were never taken. Time capture, on a tablet, on mobile or with a badge, is what makes that gap visible, and it is also what produces a defensible record, as the regulatory section below shows.

The [best time and attendance software](/en/blog/best-time-attendance-software-2026/) is distinguished exactly there: the depth of hour calculation, not the number of screens.

## Skello: the full chain without a break for shift teams

Skello approaches the problem from the opposite direction to most vendors. The product was born as a scheduling tool for sites with operational teams, then extended into time capture, absence management, HR administration and finally recruiting, with a module launched in 2026.

That lineage has a direct consequence on both handoffs. Scheduling is not a module bolted onto an HR suite: it is the foundation, and the other functions were built around it. Public plans run from 59 € per month for Time Clock Standard to 129 € per month for Duo Max, with a 10 % discount on annual commitment, and the Recruitment module is priced at 40 € per month. The detail that changes the arithmetic in seasonal businesses is the billing unit: the subscription covers the site, not the employee. A bakery that goes from four to fifteen people in summer pays the same amount in January and in August.

The figures the vendor publishes give a sense of the target cost centre: around four hours saved per week per site on scheduling, and lateness-related costs halved after time clocking is introduced. These are supplier numbers and should be read as such, but they identify the right line item, which is the site manager's administrative time. Full feature details are on the [Skello website](https://www.skello.io).

The limit is worth stating plainly: this is not a generalist HR suite. Performance reviews, skills management and training plans are not covered to the depth a suite offers.

## Rippling, Deputy, Planday and BambooHR: where each one stops

**Rippling** has the broadest scope of the panel. It combines HR, benefits, recruiting, performance, learning management, Time and Attendance and Scheduling with identity, device and inventory management on the IT side. Both handoffs exist natively, and the unified data model is the product's core argument. The trade-off is scope itself: the platform is designed for organisations that want HR, IT and payroll in one system, and its scheduling layer is not built around split shifts and sector collective agreements. Pricing is quoted on request.

**Deputy** is the closest competitor to Skello on shift work. Scheduling, shift swapping, shift planning, a time clock app and time tracking form the core, and Deputy HR adds Hire, which manages and tracks job applicants, along with new hire onboarding, engagement and document management. Both handoffs are present. A free trial runs up to 31 days with no credit card required, which makes it the easiest of the five to evaluate without a sales process.

**Planday** is a workforce management platform for shift-based businesses, with AI-assisted rotas, time tracking designed to keep payroll accurate, a communication hub replacing scattered WhatsApp threads, and compliance features. Everything downstream of hiring is well handled. There is no recruiting module, so the first handoff has to be bridged manually or with a third-party ATS.

**BambooHR** sits at the other end. Its applicant tracking system, onboarding, HR data and reporting are strong, and the platform is a reference for desk-based small and mid-sized companies. It has no shift scheduling, which rules it out as a single answer for a site with rotating teams, however good its hiring pipeline is. It remains a valid option when scheduling is genuinely not the problem, a distinction covered in more detail in this review of [staff management software](/en/blog/staff-management-software/).

## Integrated trio or assembled trio: the hidden cost

The alternative to a single platform is to connect a specialised ATS, a scheduling tool and a time tracking tool. The table below compares the two approaches on what actually gets paid for.

| Item | Integrated trio | Assembled trio |
|---|---|---|
| Functional depth per function | Good to very good on the original function, average on the others | Very good on all three |
| Connectors to maintain | None | Two at minimum |
| Possible breaking points | Zero to one | Two |
| Re-entry at every hire | None if handoff 1 is native | Five fields to retype |
| Monthly re-entry before payroll | None to minimal | A full pass if the export does not match the expected format |
| Licence cost | One subscription | Three subscriptions, usually billed per employee |
| Who to call when it breaks | One vendor | Three vendors and possibly an integrator |

The tipping point comes down to hiring volume and headcount variability. Below roughly ten joiners a year with a stable team, the assembled trio is defensible and functional depth wins. Above that, and especially in seasonal work, re-entry becomes the largest invisible cost and the single platform takes the lead.

## What regulation requires on working time records

The legal requirement explains part of the demand for this category, and it bears specifically on the time tracking function.

In its judgment of 14 May 2019 in case C-55/18, brought by the Federación de Servicios de Comisiones Obreras against Deutsche Bank SAE, the Court of Justice of the European Union held that member states must require employers to set up an objective, reliable and accessible system for measuring each worker's daily working time. The court was explicit that without such a system, neither the number of hours worked nor their distribution can be established objectively.

Practically, for any employer operating in the European Union, that rules out a roster shared over messaging plus hours recalculated by hand in a spreadsheet. It is the line between a [time tracking software](/en/blog/time-tracking-software-guide-2026/) and a simple planning tool.

## The questions to ask in a demo to test the handoffs

Four questions are enough to separate genuine integration from juxtaposition.

On the first handoff, ask: once an application is accepted, how many fields have to be typed before the person appears in the schedule with their contract, hourly rate and availability? The expected answer is zero. Any other answer prices the break.

On the second, ask to see the actual payroll export, in the target provider's format, not a dashboard screenshot. An export generated live during the demo settles the question.

On record reliability, ask how the platform handles a gap between scheduled and clocked time, and who is allowed to correct it. The answer reveals whether the tool records real hours or settles for the theoretical roster.

On sector rules, ask which alerts fire when a maximum working period is exceeded or a rest period falls short. Platforms designed for shift teams build these checks in; generalist suites treat them as configuration, which lengthens implementation. The same logic applies when choosing [recruitment software for hospitality](/en/blog/recruitment-software-hospitality/), where compliance weighs as much as sourcing speed.
