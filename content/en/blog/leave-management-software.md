**Leave management software** automates absence requests, balance calculations and manager approvals. It replaces paper forms or shared spreadsheets with a centralized tool, accessible to employees and HR teams alike, often from a mobile app.

## What is leave management software

Leave management software covers three linked functions: an employee submitting an absence request, its approval by one or several levels of management, and the automatic update of the corresponding balance. It differs from a simple shared calendar through its ability to apply accrual and deduction rules specific to each leave type.

The tool generally covers several absence categories: statutory paid leave, RTT (reduced working time days), seniority leave, leave for family events, or time-off-in-lieu. Each type follows its own accrual rules, which makes manual calculation increasingly error-prone as headcount or contract diversity grows.

In France, statutory paid leave accrues at a rate of 2.5 working days per month of actual work, amounting to 5 weeks a year for a full-time employee over a complete year. A collective agreement or company agreement can add extra days (seniority, staggered leave), which multiplies the counters to track in parallel. Dedicated software absorbs this complexity by automatically applying the right rule to each employee, without manual intervention from the HR team.

## Key features of leave management software

Four features consistently appear across market solutions. The **approval workflow** lets an employee submit a request in a few clicks and a manager approve or reject it from a notification, without email back-and-forth. **Automatic balance calculation** applies accrual rules (monthly, yearly, pro rata) and deducts approved days in real time.

The **team calendar** gives a shared view of approved absences, useful for anticipating understaffing periods before approving a new request. Finally, **payroll export** automatically transmits approved days to the payroll software or to [time tracking software](/en/blog/time-tracking-software-guide-2026/), avoiding double entry between the two tools.

Some solutions add a module for managing sick leave and supporting documents, or a direct integration with [scheduling software](/en/blog/employee-scheduling-software/) to automatically reflect an approved absence on already published shifts.

A mobile app is now part of the expected baseline: it lets employees check their balance and submit a request from their phone, and lets managers approve within seconds without returning to their desk. HR reporting completes the picture by aggregating absenteeism rates, days consumed per department and counter anomalies, indicators that later feed social reports and annual negotiations.

## Free leave management software: what actually changes

Several vendors offer a free plan limited in user count or configurable leave types, sufficient for a very small structure with simple rules. These plans cover basic requests and approvals, but quickly hit limits once the organization adds specific absence types (seniority leave, sick child days, RTT under a flat-rate agreement).

The most frequent limitation concerns balance calculation: free versions often apply a generic rule rather than the exact provisions of the applicable collective agreement. HR then has to manually recheck counters, which cancels out part of the time saved. An open-source tool like Jorani avoids licensing fees but requires in-house installation and technical maintenance, a cost not to overlook for a small structure.

An Excel spreadsheet remains a widespread free alternative, but it automates neither the approval workflow nor balance calculation: every rule change or contract update requires a manual formula fix, with a growing risk of error as the file is shared among several people. Switching to a dedicated tool generally becomes worthwhile once a company passes about ten employees or manages more than two different leave types.

## Comparing leave management software

The table below compares the main solutions available on the French market, without any ranking: the right choice depends on the company's profile and its related needs (payroll, scheduling, time and attendance).

| Software | Positioning | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Lucca (Figgo) | Modular HRIS | Complex rules, multi-agreement support |
| Eurécia | General HRIS | Leave module built into a full HRIS |
| Skello | Field scheduling | Leave directly linked to team scheduling |
| Factorial | General HRIS | Simple interface, good feature-to-price ratio |
| Jorani | Open source | Free, hosting and maintenance handled in-house |
| MyCongés | SMB-focused | Fast onboarding for small structures |

This comparison remains indicative: pricing and feature scope change regularly, testing against the company's actual use cases remains necessary before any commitment.

Two criteria help narrow this choice quickly. The first is the complexity of the collective agreement rules to apply: a company under a single, simple collective agreement can manage with a general tool, while a multi-agreement or multi-site organization should check the software's ability to handle several rule sets in parallel without manual per-site configuration. The second is whether a scheduling module is needed alongside leave: field teams with variable hours benefit from a tool that handles leave and scheduling in the same interface, rather than two separate systems to keep in sync.

## How to choose leave management software by company size

A small business with stable rules and a limited headcount can be satisfied with a leave module built into a general HRIS, without needing a dedicated tool. The deciding factor then becomes ease of use rather than depth of configuration.

A mid-sized or larger multi-site company, on the other hand, benefits from prioritizing a tool able to manage several collective agreements and several absence types in parallel, with automated payroll export. This applies to teams already using [time and attendance software](/en/blog/best-time-attendance-software-2026/) to track working time: the leave module then needs to integrate natively to avoid discrepancies between the two systems.

A company looking to replace an existing tool that has become too rigid, for example after comparing [Kelio alternatives](/en/blog/kelio-alternatives/), should first check how balance history is carried over during migration: a poor transfer of counters at the start of a contract is the leading source of disputes with employees.

The level of integration with existing tools matters as much as the leave module's own features. A standalone tool that exchanges no data with payroll or scheduling eventually recreates the same double entry as the spreadsheet it was meant to replace. Conversely, a solution connected to the rest of the HRIS automatically transmits every approved leave to payslips and to team schedules, removing the risk of discrepancy between the counters shown to employees and those actually processed by administrators.
