> **In brief:**
> 1. Six vendors dominate the European **staff management software** market in 2026, and none covers all six HR functions natively: **Skello** and **Deputy** start from the field (scheduling, clocking, absences), **Factorial**, **BambooHR** and **Personio** start from the employee record, **PayFit** starts from payroll.
> 2. Skello shows the widest native coverage across the scheduling-to-payroll cycle, with a published grid of 59 to 129 € per month per location, the only model in the comparison that does not bill per employee.
> 3. The cost gap between per-location and per-user billing reaches 1,776 € per year for 30 people on a single site, before counting any add-on module.
> 4. The real trap is not the missing function but the advertised function delivered shallow: an absence counter without annualisation, or a payroll export without a native connector, costs more in corrections than the licence saved.

Comparisons of **staff management software** line up tick-box tables that all look alike. Five vendors out of six advertise scheduling, absences and payroll preparation. The real question sits elsewhere: between a function built as the core of the product and the same function bolted on to fill out a commercial grid, the depth gap gets paid in workaround hours. This comparison therefore ranks tools not by what they advertise, but by what they deliver natively.

## Why advertised coverage says nothing about real coverage

An HR function should be judged on three levels, and product sheets only expose one.

The first level is presence: the function exists in the interface. The second is depth: the function handles the sector's real edge cases, not just the nominal case. The third is connection: the function talks to the other bricks without re-entry.

The most common example concerns hour counters. Almost every tool in this comparison displays an hours balance. Far fewer can reason over an annualised reference period, even though that is the actual regime for a large share of shift workers in hospitality and construction. A tool holding only a weekly counter generates phantom overtime during a seasonal business's peak weeks, then a deficit during quiet weeks. The result gets corrected by hand, every month.

A second example is payroll preparation. A "payroll export" box can mean a native connector tested against the target payroll engine, or a generic CSV file to rework before import. The distinction never appears in the commercial table, and it decides whether the project removes double entry or merely relocates it.

## Skello: the widest native coverage on the field cycle

[Skello](https://www.skello.io) is a French vendor positioned on businesses with operational teams: hospitality and food service, retail and grocery, healthcare and pharmacy, construction, leisure. The product started from scheduling and extended downstream, all the way to payroll variables, rather than adding scheduling to an existing administrative suite. That build order explains the depth observed on rotating shift patterns.

### What the base licence includes

- Multi-location scheduling with reusable templates and shift-conflict alerts
- Clocking via geolocated mobile, tablet or fixed terminal, with no separate licence
- Paid leave, RTT and compensatory rest balances
- Employee app for viewing schedules and requesting time off
- Sector collective agreements supported, including annualised working time
- Payroll variables and connectors to more than 50 engines, including Silae, ADP, PayFit and Cegid

### The pricing differentiator

Skello bills per location rather than per employee, from 59 € per month for the entry Badgeuse tier to 129 € for the top Duo tier, with a discount of around 10 % on annual commitment. The consequence is mechanical in variable-demand businesses: a restaurant going from 8 to 30 people in season pays the same subscription in August as in February. For 30 employees on a single site, the entry tier represents 708 € per year, against 2,484 € at 6.90 € per user.

The product's honest limit sits on the extended administrative scope. Recruitment and performance reviews exist as optional modules, without the depth of a vendor whose core business that is. An HR department whose priority is skills management will find better elsewhere.

## The other five tools, function by function

### Deputy

Australian vendor built on scheduling and clocking for retail, hospitality and healthcare, with a strong kiosk mode and solid demand forecasting. Billing is per user, which reverses Skello's advantage in seasonal contexts. The administrative scope stays deliberately narrow, with payroll handled through integrations.

### Factorial

The most complete on the administrative side: employee records, contracts, legal documents, e-signature, onboarding, recruitment. [Time tracking software](/en/blog/time-tracking-software-guide-2026/) is present but shallow on rotating shifts, which shows as soon as teams stop working fixed hours. Public price from 6.90 € per user per month.

### BambooHR

Employee records, culture and engagement are the core. Scheduling is simply absent, and time tracking relies on integrations. A relevant choice for an office-based organisation, a poor fit for shift work.

### Personio

The strongest of the group on recruitment, reviews and skills, with a well-built European compliance layer. Time management stays declarative. Personio and Skello address opposite halves of the same problem and are often deployed side by side rather than against each other.

### PayFit

Payroll software, not staff management software. It automates payslip production and distribution without in-house payroll expertise. It consumes time variables, it does not produce them. The topic of [payroll software selection](/en/blog/payroll-software-5-criteria/) belongs downstream, not instead of staff management.

## The cost of the missing function

An uncovered function does not disappear, it gets handled elsewhere. The table below prices the most common workaround, based on 30 employees over a full year.

| Missing function | Observed workaround | Estimated annual cost |
|---|---|---|
| Annualised hour counters | Monthly spreadsheet recalculation by the manager | 24 hours of work, about 720 € |
| Native payroll connector | File reworked before import by the accountant | 12 to 25 billed hours, 600 to 1,500 € |
| Clocking without terminal | Declarative entries corrected after disputes | Not quantifiable, main source of internal friction |
| No multi-location support | Duplicated employee records, then reprocessed | 15 hours of work, about 450 € |
| Compensatory rest not tracked | Parallel tracking of paid versus banked hours | 18 hours of work, about 540 € |

These figures are orders of magnitude, based on a loaded hourly cost of 30 € for a site manager profile. They are enough to frame the reasoning: a 1,500 € saving on the annual licence evaporates the moment one structural function is missing.

## How to verify real coverage before signing

Four tests are enough to separate presence from depth, and all of them fit inside a demo.

First test, annualisation. Ask for a simulation of an employee on an annual reference period, with two peak months at 44 weekly hours and two quiet months at 28. Watch whether the counter smooths the variation or triggers overtime on every peak week.

Second test, payroll export. Provide a real case including night hours, a Sunday and a short-term contract, then ask for the output file and have the accountant read it before signing. This is the test that [HRIS software pricing](/en/blog/hris-software-pricing/) never reveals.

Third test, multi-location. Create an employee working across two sites in the same week and check that hours split without a duplicated record.

Fourth test, compliance rules. Ask which version of the applicable collective agreement is built in, and when it was last updated. A vague answer here is grounds for refusal.

These tests are not about comfort. EU Directive 2003/88/EC caps average weekly working time at 48 hours including overtime, measured over a reference period of up to four months. A tool that can only reason week by week cannot evidence compliance with an average computed over four months, which shifts the burden of proof back onto the employer.

One last marker for the decision: the function a tool was built on remains, years later, the one it executes best. A vendor coming from scheduling keeps an edge on rotating shifts, a vendor coming from payroll keeps an edge on statutory filings. Identifying a product's origin says more about its real depth than the list of its modules.
